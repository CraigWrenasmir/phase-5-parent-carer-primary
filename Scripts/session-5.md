### Slide 3 - Building a home-school partnership

**Segment: `s05_sl03_a`**

The Family-School Partnerships Framework recognises parents and families as children's first and continuing educators. Learning happens across settings, and a family's diversity and strengths matter to that learning.

The other principles include community involvement, mutual trust and shared responsibility, supported by committed and collaborative leadership. In an everyday conversation, look for respectful listening, clear information, your child's views and agreed next steps. Family knowledge and school knowledge are both needed from the beginning. Respectful listening is possible even when people see a situation differently.

### Slide 5 - Power dynamics

**Segment: `s05_sl06_a`**

Power can sit in the way a meeting is arranged, the language used and who can make decisions. Familiarity with school systems, cultural and historical experiences of education, language and financial circumstances can also shape how a conversation feels.

Who sets the agenda? Whose knowledge gets heard? Is your child included in a way that works for them? What might need to change about the process? Naming these features can help us plan a more balanced conversation. We can examine the meeting itself without turning the discussion into a judgement about an identifiable person.

### Slide 9 - Consultation literacy

**Segment: `s05_sl10_a`**

In Session One, we looked at your rights at school. Under the Disability Standards for Education, the school must consult your child, or you on their behalf, about reasonable adjustments. This checklist helps make that consultation useful.

Consultation is more useful when there is a clear shared purpose, open questions and more than one way to contribute. Your knowledge and your child's knowledge deserve attention. What was heard needs to be recorded accurately, followed by an agreed action, responsibility and follow-up.

The checklist on page sixteen can help before and after a meeting. Ask, listen and follow up. Before leaving, check who will do what and when you will hear how it is going. A conversation becomes more useful when everyone understands the next step.

### Slide 10 - Partnership tools

**Segment: `s05_sl11_a`**

The Working Together Communication Tool, on the left, helps you agree how home and school will communicate. You can choose a method, such as email, phone or a communication book, then record how often, what you'll communicate about, and the next dates or steps.

The Partnership Planning Template, on the right, asks what has worked, what has been challenging and what could help. You can use those prompts before a meeting or complete them together. A tool gives the conversation a place to start. Keep listening and your child's own communication alongside it.
